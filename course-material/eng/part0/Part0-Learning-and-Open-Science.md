# Before You Learn: Studying and Contributing to an Open Course

This course connects chemical structure, biological function, measurement, and computational conclusions step by step. The core material is in Markdown chapters. Each chapter begins with a subject-specific question and ends with exercises.

## Working Through a Chapter

Read the introduction and state the question you are about to investigate. Follow the diagram and explanation, then reproduce the numerical example. Before running provided code, write down your prediction. After running it, explain why the result came out that way.

Each chapter ends with questions that test understanding and exercises that apply it. A star marks an extension. If you get stuck, write down the known quantities, assumptions, and the step you do not understand. This is more useful than immediately looking up an answer.

## Sources and Results

A paper citation identifies the origin of an idea or observation. Check what was studied, what was measured, and under which conditions. Teaching values in this course are labeled explicitly; they must not be presented as experimental properties of a drug.

In your own notes, distinguish observations, assumptions, and conclusions. If you use someone else's code or figure, give its source and conditions of use. When working with an AI assistant, remain responsible for explaining and checking the result.

## Working Openly

An error report is useful when another person can locate the problem: specify the chapter, passage, expected result, and basis for the correction. For a computational error, include the command and environment version.

See the [PR requirements](../../../community/eng/PR_requirement.md) for the contribution process. Attempt the exercises yourself first; [solution discussions](../../../community/eng/Exercise-Discussions.md) are available separately.
